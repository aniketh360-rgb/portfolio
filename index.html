Bachelor’s thesis

THWS · B.Eng. · 2026

Business Models for Refurbished Capital Equipment

A Study of Trade-In and Buyback Strategies

When a semiconductor test system leaves its first owner, it can still have value for the next one. I studied how a supplier can recover that value, and what would make an equipment owner willing to participate.

Focus

I compared trade-in, where the owner receives credit towards another purchase, with buyback, where the supplier purchases the used asset separately. I focused on customer purchasing behaviour, commercial feasibility and equipment reuse in a B2B market.

Approach

I used qualitative expert interviews and thematic analysis to connect industry experience with the literature. Customer journey mapping helped me examine where an owner decides whether to return equipment. PESTEL and circular-economy frameworks placed that decision in its wider market context.

Outcome

The thesis brought the two models into a practical comparison. A trade-in ties equipment recovery to the customer’s next purchase. A buyback can recover equipment even when the owner is not replacing it. I used that distinction to explain how customer timing, configuration and refurbishment cost shape the choice.
